How to read the board.

Colors flag what's unusual
Above prediction

Tide tables only know the moon and sun. Storms, low pressure and this winter's El Niño push the water higher. Over 9 inches is amber and over 1.5 ft red: the beach gets narrower and logs can float.

Air pressure

Normal is about 1013 mb. Lower means stormier weather, and it lifts the sea: every millibar below normal adds about 0.4 inch to the tide. Under 1000 is red, 1000–1009 amber. Falling pressure means weather is moving in.

Gusts and waves

Gusts over 20 mph are amber and over 25 red. Waves come from the New Dungeness buoy: 1–3 ft amber, over 3 ft red. At Fort Worden, wind from the north or west drives waves onto the beach; wind from the south or east flattens it but pushes floaters out.

Your call

The colors flag what's unusual, not a rule: green is typical, amber is worth noticing, red is well outside normal. Whether to go in is up to you. Look at the beach too: logs moving at the waterline, waves at the top of the beach, a fresh bluff slide, lightning or failing light.

Temperature

Compared with normal for the date over the last 10 years. Water barely varies, so 1.5° off is amber and 3° red; air swings more, so 5° and 10°. Warmer or colder both count.
