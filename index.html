How to read the board

Above prediction

Tide tables only know the moon and sun. Storms, low pressure and this winter's El Niño push the water higher. More than 6 inches over the tables is amber; more than a foot is red, when logs start to float.

Gusts and waves

Gusts over 20 mph are amber and over 25 red. Waves come from the New Dungeness buoy: 1–3 ft amber, over 3 ft red. At Fort Worden, wind from the north or west drives waves onto the beach; wind from the south or east flattens it but pushes floaters out.

Any red means no plunge

The bands are the group's rules of thumb, not official safety limits. Trust your eyes too: logs moving at the waterline, waves at the top of the beach, a fresh bluff slide, lightning or failing light all mean out.

Look back

Use the date box to see any day this month. Past days show what was measured; days ahead show predicted tides only. The board rebuilds every morning before 6 am.
